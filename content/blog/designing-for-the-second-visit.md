title: Designing for the second visit
date: 2026-09-18
summary: First impressions get the attention, but the products people keep are the ones that feel better the second time.
---

Most design reviews judge a screen the way a stranger would see it: once, with fresh eyes, for a few seconds. That matters. But the products I come back to are the ones that get quieter as I learn them.

## What changes on the second visit

On a second visit people aren't exploring any more. They have a task, and they want the shortest path to it. Onboarding hints become noise. A clever animation becomes a delay.

A few questions I now ask in every review:

- What does this screen look like to someone who has seen it fifty times?
- Which element is only here to explain the product, and can it step aside later?
- Is the most common action also the easiest one to reach?

## Earning a place in someone's day

Good tools fade into habit. That is a harder brief than looking impressive, and a more useful one.
